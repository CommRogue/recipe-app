# Store compliance for a public release

- **Issue:** [#13 — Store compliance for a public release](https://github.com/CommRogue/recipe-app/issues/13)
- **Question:** What must a public iOS and Android release of an AI recipe app with accounts and subscriptions satisfy? (Sign in with Apple when offering Google sign-in; in-app account deletion; privacy policy and where it must be linked; Apple privacy nutrition labels and Play Data safety form; subscription disclosure rules; rules about AI-generated content or health/nutrition claims.)
- **Researched:** 2026-09-18. All sources are first-party pages fetched on that date. Versions read: Apple App Review Guidelines "Last Updated: June 8, 2026"; Google Play Developer Program Policy "effective August 26, 2026".
- **Not legal advice.** This covers store rules only, not GDPR/CCPA/consumer law, which the stores also require you to follow.

## Summary

Nothing in either store forbids this app, but several rules shape v1 design, not just the submission form. (1) Offering Google sign-in on iOS triggers Apple 4.8: an equivalent privacy-preserving login (in practice Sign in with Apple) must sit beside it, and deleting an account then also requires revoking the Apple token. (2) Both stores require real account deletion started inside the app; Google additionally requires a public web URL where a user who has uninstalled the app can request deletion. With client-direct Firestore (ADR 0001) something has to delete the user's Recipes, Collections, Ratings, Cooking Sessions, Cover photos and backend Quota records, not just the Firebase Auth user. (3) Apple 5.1.2(i) now names "third-party AI" explicitly: sending personal data to a third-party AI needs clear disclosure and explicit permission first, which touches every Generation Request. (4) Google's AI-Generated Content policy requires an in-app way to report or flag offensive generated content. (5) Google's Health apps declaration has a "Nutrition and Weight Management" feature that plausibly covers this app; if declared, the listing needs the "not a medical device" disclaimer and the app must tell users to consult a healthcare professional. (6) The biggest schedule risks are procedural: a new personal Play account must run a closed test with 12 testers for 14 continuous days before production access; iOS builds must be made with Xcode 26 (needs macOS, which the developer does not have locally); new Android apps must target API 36.

Legend: **[MUST]** = stated as a requirement in the cited source. **[SHOULD]** = recommendation, either the source's own "should" or this document's reading of how a rule applies to this app.

## Checklist

### 1. Sign-in

- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** If the iOS app offers Google sign-in (or any third-party/social login) for the user's primary account, also offer "as an equivalent option another login service" that (a) limits data collection to name and email, (b) lets the user keep their email private, and (c) does not collect app interactions for advertising without consent. Sign in with Apple is the login that meets this; the guideline no longer names it, so another qualifying service is technically allowed. — Guideline 4.8, [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#login-services)
- [ ] **[Apple]** Exemption to know about: 4.8 does not apply if the app "exclusively uses your company's own account setup and sign-in systems" (e.g. email/password only, no Google button on iOS). — Guideline 4.8, same link
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** Do not force login for features that do not need an account: "If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login." Saved Recipes, sync, Quota and Plan are account-based, so a login wall is defensible, but say why in the review notes. — Guideline 5.1.1(v), [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage)
- [ ] **[Google]** No equivalent of 4.8 exists in Play policy; Sign in with Apple is not required on Android. (Absence confirmed by reading the full Developer Program Policy text; see Sources.)
- [ ] **[Both] [SHOULD]** If Sign in with Apple is offered on Android/web too, it needs a Services ID and OAuth code flow configured in the Firebase Apple provider. The same configuration is a prerequisite for token revocation (section 2). — [Firebase: Authenticate Using Apple (iOS), "Token revocation"](https://firebase.google.com/docs/auth/ios/apple)

### 2. Account deletion

- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** "If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app." — Guideline 5.1.1(v), [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage)
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** Deletion must be easy to find (typically account settings), must delete "the entire account record, along with associated personal data"; "only offering to temporarily deactivate or disable an account is insufficient." If a website finishes the process, link directly to that page. Apps outside highly regulated industries "should not require people to make a phone call, send an email, or go through other support flows." A confirmation step or re-authentication is fine; a slow/manual backend process is fine if you tell the user how long it takes and confirm completion. — [Offering account deletion in your app](https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/)
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** "Apps that support Sign in with Apple should use the Sign in with Apple REST API to revoke user tokens" when deleting the account. With Firebase Auth in Flutter this is `FirebaseAuth.instance.revokeTokenWithAuthorizationCode(authCode)`; Firebase does not store the Apple tokens, so the user must sign in with Apple again immediately before deletion to obtain a fresh authorization code. If no token or code is available you must still delete the account and direct the user to revoke manually. — [Offering account deletion](https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/); [TN3194](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3194-handling-account-deletions-and-revoking-tokens-for-sign-in-with-apple); [Firebase Flutter federated auth, "Revoke Apple auth tokens"](https://firebase.google.com/docs/auth/flutter/federated-auth); [Firebase iOS Apple, "Token revocation"](https://firebase.google.com/docs/auth/ios/apple)
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** For a user with an active Subscription: "notify them that their billing will continue through Apple and request that they cancel their subscription before continuing", and offer the manage-subscriptions sheet or link. — [Offering account deletion, FAQ](https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/)
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** "If your app allows users to create an account from within your app, then it must also allow users to request for their account to be deleted. Users must have a readily discoverable option to initiate app account deletion from within your app and outside of your app (for example, by visiting your website). A link to this web resource must be entered in the designated URL form field within Play Console." — User Data policy, Account Deletion Requirement, [Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17190352?hl=en)
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** The web resource must work for someone who has uninstalled the app (it must not send them back to the app), load without error, feature the deletion path prominently, and "reference the app or developer name" as shown on the store listing. A form or a support email on that page is acceptable to Google. If the user must do something first (e.g. cancel a Subscription), the page must say so. — [Understanding Google Play's app account deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111?hl=en)
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** Deleting the account means deleting the associated user data; "Temporary account deactivation, disabling, or 'freezing' the app account does not qualify." Data kept for "security, fraud prevention, or regulatory compliance" must be disclosed (e.g. in the privacy policy). Data held by service providers should be deleted there too. — same two sources
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** Answer the Data deletion questions in the Data safety form, including the deletion URL; a new app cannot be published with these incomplete. — [app account deletion requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111?hl=en)
- [ ] **[Both] [SHOULD]** Implementation note for this architecture: `user.delete()` in Firebase Auth removes only the auth record and fails with `requires-recent-login` if the session is old, so the flow needs re-authentication. Firestore documents (profile, Recipes, Collections, Ratings, Cooking Sessions), Cover photos in storage, and backend Quota/Plan records must be deleted separately. — [Firebase Flutter: Manage users, "Delete a user" / "Re-authenticate a user"](https://firebase.google.com/docs/auth/flutter/manage-users)

### 3. Privacy policy

- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** "All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app in an easily accessible manner." The policy must identify what data is collected, how, and all uses; confirm that third parties receiving data (SDKs, analytics, and here Google as the AI/model and hosting provider) provide equal protection; and "explain its data retention/deletion policies and describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion." — Guideline 5.1.1(i), [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage)
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** "All apps must post a privacy policy link in the designated field within Play Console, and a privacy policy link or text within the app itself." The URL must be "active, publicly accessible and non-geofenced … (no PDFs) and … non-editable". It must include developer information and a privacy point of contact, the types of personal and sensitive data accessed/collected/used/shared and with whom, secure data handling procedures, and the data retention and deletion policy. — User Data policy, [Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17190352?hl=en) (standalone page: [User Data](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311?hl=en))
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** Because the app sells auto-renewing Subscriptions: "Links to Your Privacy Policy and Terms of Use must be accessible within Your Licensed Application", and Apple's subscription guidance adds "your app and App Store metadata must include links to your Terms of Use and Privacy Policy." So a Terms of Use document is also needed (Apple's standard EULA can serve if you do not write your own), linked in the app (put both on the paywall and in settings) and in the App Store listing. — Schedule 2 §3.8(b), [Apple Developer Program License Agreement](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/); [Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)
- [ ] **[Both] [SHOULD]** Link the policy from the sign-in screen as well as settings, so it is reachable before an account exists. Host policy, terms, and the account-deletion page on one small public site.

### 4. Privacy labels (Apple) and Data safety (Google)

- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** Complete App Privacy details in App Store Connect before submission. You must declare all data "you or your third-party partners collect", where "collect" means transmitting data off the device and keeping it longer than needed to service the request in real time. Data used only for app functionality still has to be declared. — [App privacy details on the App Store](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)
- [ ] **[Apple] [SHOULD]** Expected declarations for this app, all "linked to the user", purpose App Functionality, not used for tracking: Contact Info (email, name from the sign-in provider), Identifiers (User ID), User Content (Recipes, Ratings notes, free-text asks in a Generation Request; Photos if a Cover photo is uploaded), Purchases (Subscription status), plus Diagnostics/Usage Data only if Crashlytics/Analytics are added. Decide whether allergy Constraints count as "Health — … any other user provided health or medical data" (see Open questions). — data type definitions in [App privacy details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/); what each Firebase SDK collects in [Firebase: Apple platforms data collection](https://firebase.google.com/docs/ios/app-store-data-collection)
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** Ship privacy manifests. Apps that use required-reason APIs without declaring approved reasons in `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` "aren't accepted by App Store Connect" (since 2024-05-01). Apple's list of SDKs that must carry their own manifest and signature includes `Flutter`, `FirebaseAuth`, `FirebaseCore`, `FirebaseFirestore`, `FirebaseInstallations`, `GoogleSignIn`, `GoogleUtilities`, `shared_preferences_ios`, `path_provider`, `image_picker_ios`, `package_info_plus` and others likely in this app, so use SDK versions that bundle manifests. — [Describing use of required reason API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/describing-use-of-required-reason-api); [Third-party SDK requirements](https://developer.apple.com/support/third-party-SDK-requirements/); [Privacy manifest files](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/privacy-manifest-files)
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** No App Tracking Transparency prompt is needed unless the app tracks (links its data with third-party data for advertising or shares with data brokers). If ads or ad attribution SDKs are ever added, ATT permission is required first. — Guideline 5.1.2(i), [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-use-and-sharing)
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** Complete the Data safety form. It is required for every app on closed, open or production tracks (only internal testing is exempt), so it is due before the mandatory closed test, not at launch. It needs a privacy policy URL, and must be "consistent with the disclosures made in the app's privacy policy." — [Provide information for Google Play's Data safety section](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469?hl=en); User Data policy, [Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17190352?hl=en)
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** Declare data collected by SDKs as well as your own code ("irrespective of whether data is transmitted to you or a third-party server"). Transfers to a service provider processing on your behalf (Firebase, Cloud Run, Vertex AI under your Google Cloud terms) are not "sharing" in the form, but are still "collection". Pseudonymous data must be declared. Data processed only ephemerally is entered in the form but not shown on the listing. — [Data safety section help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469?hl=en)
- [ ] **[Google] [SHOULD]** Expected answers: collects Personal info (name, email, user IDs), Photos (if Cover photos), App activity (other user-generated content, in-app search/asks), Purchase history; possibly Health info (allergy Constraints; see Open questions). Encrypted in transit: yes (Firebase states it encrypts in transit over HTTPS; the Go service must be HTTPS-only). Users can request deletion: yes, with the URL from section 2. — [Firebase: Play data disclosure](https://firebase.google.com/docs/android/play-data-disclosure)
- [ ] **[Both] [MUST]** Keep both declarations current; both stores hold the developer responsible for accuracy, including for third-party SDK behaviour. Apple answers can be changed without an app update.

### 5. Subscriptions

- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** Unlocking the Paid Plan inside the iOS app must use In-App Purchase. — Guideline 3.1.1, [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#in-app-purchase); Schedule 2 §3.11, [License Agreement](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** Sign the Paid Apps Agreement (and complete tax/banking) before any In-App Purchase can be offered. The first auto-renewable Subscription and its subscription group "must be submitted with a new app version", so the Subscription is reviewed together with the first binary that contains the paywall. — [Sign and update agreements](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-agreements/sign-and-update-agreements/); [Offer auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/offer-auto-renewable-subscriptions/)
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** The Subscription must give ongoing value, last at least seven days, and work on all of the user's devices. A Paid Plan with a recurring Quota and a higher cap on saved Recipes fits; "Subscriptions may include consumable credits". — Guideline 3.1.2(a), [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#subscriptions)
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** On the paywall, "clearly and conspicuously disclose": the title of the Subscription, its length, its price (and price per unit if appropriate), with Privacy Policy and Terms of Use links accessible in the app. Apple's subscription guidance says the sign-up screen must include the subscription name and duration, what is provided during the period, the "full renewal price, shown clearly and prominently, and localized", and "a way for current subscribers to sign in or restore purchases". The amount actually billed must be the most prominent price; a per-month equivalent of an annual price must be subordinate. For free trials, state the trial length and the price billed afterwards. — Guideline 3.1.2(c); Schedule 2 §3.8(b), [License Agreement](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/); [Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** Describe before purchase what the user gets for the price (state the Quota and Recipe cap of each Plan concretely). Do not require extra tasks to get what was paid for. Avoid letting a user subscribe to two variants of the same thing (put monthly and annual in one subscription group). — Guidelines 3.1.2(a)–(c), same link
- [ ] **[Apple] [SHOULD]** Provide a restore mechanism and an in-app route to manage the Subscription (`showManageSubscriptions`). — Guideline 3.1.1 ("you should make sure you have a restore mechanism"); [Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** If the Paid Plan is ever sold on the web too, the app may honour it (3.1.3(b) Multiplatform Services) only if it is also available as an In-App Purchase. Outside the United States storefront, the app and its metadata may not contain buttons, links or calls to action pointing to other purchase methods without an entitlement. — Guidelines 3.1.1(a), 3.1.3, 3.1.3(b), [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#other-purchase-methods)
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** Selling the Paid Plan in the Android app must use Google Play's billing system, and outside the listed programs the app "may not lead users to a payment method other than Google Play's billing system". Alternative billing and external-offer programs exist for eligible countries/regions and require enrolment. — Payments policy, [Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17190352?hl=en)
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** "You must be transparent about your offer. This includes clearly and explicitly disclosing your offer terms, the cost of your subscription, the frequency of your billing cycle, the automatic renewal terms, whether a subscription is required to use the app, and any other material information about the subscription. Users should not have to perform any additional action to review the information." Listed violations to avoid: not saying the Subscription auto-renews; an annual Subscription that most prominently shows a monthly price; incompletely localised pricing and terms; a paywall without a clearly visible dismiss button or that does not show the app is usable on the Free Plan; product names like "Free Trial" for an auto-renewing product. — [Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en)
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** For free trials and introductory offers, state duration, price, what is included, "how and when a free trial will convert to a paid subscription, how much the paid subscription will cost, and how a user can cancel." — same source
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** "Clearly disclose how a user can manage or cancel their subscription" and "include in your app access to an easy-to-use, online method to cancel the subscription"; a link to Google Play's Subscription Center in account settings satisfies this. — same source
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** A Subscription must provide "sustained or recurring value"; it may not be used for "lump sum in-app credits". A Quota that renews each period is recurring value; do not sell one-off generation packs as a Subscription (use an in-app product for that). In-app prices must match the Play billing sheet, and the store listing must say that some features require payment. — [Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en); Payments policy
- [ ] **[Both] [SHOULD]** Plan/Quota enforcement already lives in the backend (ADR 0001 consequences); it needs server-side verification of store purchases to grant the Paid Plan. Not a store rule, but reviewers on both stores must be able to complete or see the purchase flow in sandbox.

### 6. AI-generated content

- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** "Apps that generate content using AI must contain in-app user reporting or flagging features that allow users to report or flag offensive content to developers without needing to exit the app. Developers should utilize user reports to inform content filtering and moderation." Add a "Report" action on every Draft (and on saved Recipes that came from a Draft) that sends the content and Generation Request to you. — AI-Generated Content policy, [Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17190352?hl=en)
- [ ] **[Google]** Scope note: Google's explainer lists chatbots, image generators and voice/video of real people as covered "including but not limited to", and excludes only apps that merely host AI content, pure summarisers, and "productivity apps that use AI to improve an existing feature". A recipe generator with free-text asks and Refinements is not in an excluded group, so treat the policy as applying. The app must also prevent generation of Restricted Content (e.g. harmful or dangerous instructions via the free-text ask). — [Understanding Google Play's AI-Generated Content policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14094294?hl=en); [Best Practices to Safeguard AI-Generated Content](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16353813?hl=en)
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so." A Generation Request carries the user's Constraints, Preferences and free text to Gemini on Vertex AI. Before the first generation, show a disclosure naming the AI provider and what is sent, and get an explicit tap to agree; record it in the privacy policy too. Minimise what is sent (no email, name or UID in the prompt). — Guideline 5.1.2(i), [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-use-and-sharing)
- [ ] **[Apple]** The App Review Guidelines contain no dedicated AI-generated-content section as of the June 8, 2026 text; the only explicit AI wording is in 5.1.2(i) (checked by full-text search). General rules still apply to generated output: 1.1 objectionable content, 1.4 physical harm, 2.3.1 no misleading marketing. — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- [ ] **[Apple] [SHOULD]** A "Report" action also helps on iOS. If recipe sharing (in `plan.md`) ever makes one user's content visible to other users inside the app, Apple 1.2 and Google's User Generated Content policy then require filtering, reporting, blocking of abusive users, published contact info, and (Google) acceptance of terms before posting. Sharing out via the OS share sheet does not create in-app UGC. — Guideline 1.2, [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#user-generated-content); User Generated Content policy, [Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17190352?hl=en)
- [ ] **[Both] [SHOULD]** Say in the store descriptions and in the app that Recipes are AI-generated. Neither store states this as a rule for this category of app, but Google's Misleading Claims policy and Apple 2.3.1 forbid misdescribing functionality.

### 7. Health and nutrition claims (estimated macros, Constraints)

- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** Complete the Health apps declaration in Play Console. Every app must, including apps on closed testing; apps with no health features certify that. The feature list includes "Nutrition and Weight Management: Tools for tracking dietary intake, planning meals, managing diets, and supporting weight loss or weight management goals. Also includes apps focused on specific dietary needs or goals." An app built around dietary Constraints, macro-leaning Preferences and per-Recipe macros plausibly fits; see Open questions. — [Provide information for the Health apps declaration form](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14738291?hl=en)
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST, if declared as having health features]** The Health Content and Services policy then applies: "We don't allow apps with health and medical related functionalities that are misleading or potentially harmful"; non-medical-device health apps "must include a clear disclaimer in their app description indicating that the app is 'not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition'", and "Apps must also remind users to consult a healthcare professional for medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment." The privacy policy must cover health-related data with limited-use wording. — Health Content and Services, [Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17190352?hl=en); [Health app categories and additional information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13996367?hl=en)
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** No health misinformation and no misleading claims in listing or app. Do not describe macros as exact, and do not claim a Recipe is "safe for" an allergy or treats a condition. — Health Content and Services; Misleading Claims, same policy page
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** Guideline 1.4: "If your app behaves in a way that risks physical harm, we may reject it." 1.4.1 targets medical apps: "Apps must clearly disclose data and methodology to support accuracy claims relating to health measurements", and "Apps should remind users to check with a doctor in addition to using the app and before making medical decisions." The app is not a medical app if it makes no medical claims, so keep it that way: present macros as estimates everywhere they appear, state how they are estimated (by the model, not measured or lab-verified), and make no accuracy claim. — Guidelines 1.4, 1.4.1, [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#physical-harm)
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** Stay out of "highly regulated fields (such as … healthcare …)": those apps "should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer." Marketing the app as a diet-treatment, allergy-management or medical-nutrition tool would put an individual developer account at risk. — Guideline 5.1.1(ix), [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage)
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST]** Answer the age-rating questionnaire honestly (2.3.6). The current questionnaire has "Health or Wellness Topics: Content that provides self-care or lifestyle recommendations. May include: calorie tracking, dieting advice, or exercise recommendations", which places an app at 9+ or higher. "Medical or Treatment Information" should be "none" for this app. — [Age ratings values and definitions](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/age-ratings-values-and-definitions/); Guideline 2.3.6
- [ ] **[Apple] [MUST, conditional]** If the app's primary or secondary category is Health & Fitness or Medical (or "frequent" Medical or Treatment Information is declared), and it is available in the EU/EEA, UK or US, you must declare whether it is a regulated medical device (answer: No). Choosing Food & Drink as the category avoids the question. — [Declare regulated medical device status](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/declare-regulated-medical-device-status/)
- [ ] **[Both] [MUST]** Do not use HealthKit or Health Connect in v1 unless needed. Both bring extra rules (Apple 5.1.3 forbids writing "false or inaccurate data into HealthKit", which estimated macros risk; Google's Health Connect permissions require an approved use case and declaration). — Guideline 5.1.3, [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#health-and-health-research); Health Connect section, [Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17190352?hl=en)
- [ ] **[Both] [SHOULD]** Allergy Constraints are the realistic physical-harm path: a Draft that violates a Constraint is "invalid" in `CONTEXT.md`, but a model can still produce one. No store rule prescribes wording, but a standing notice ("AI-generated; always check ingredients and labels yourself if you have an allergy") on Drafts and Recipes, plus server-side checking of Drafts against Constraints, addresses Apple 1.4 and Google's "potentially harmful" language. This is this document's recommendation, not a quoted requirement.

### 8. Other release blockers

**Google Play**

- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** New personal developer accounts (created after 2023-11-13) "must run a closed test for their app with a minimum of 12 testers who have been opted in continuously for at least 14 days" before they can apply for production access; the application then asks questions about the test. Closed testing itself requires completed app setup (Data safety, Health declaration, content rating, privacy policy, etc.). Organisation accounts are not subject to this but require a D-U-N-S number. Plan at least 3–4 weeks of lead time and recruit more than 12 testers, because dropping below 12 opted-in breaks the continuous period. — [App testing requirements for new personal developer accounts](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en)
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** Verify developer identity: government ID for a personal account, a verified developer email (shown publicly on Google Play), and device verification for individual accounts before phone verification. What is displayed publicly depends on account type and on whether you monetise. — [Verify your developer identity information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10841920?hl=en)
- [ ] **[Google] [MUST]** Target API level: since 2026-08-31 new apps "must target Android 16 (API level 36) or higher". — [Target API level requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11926878?hl=en)

- [ ] **[Both] [MUST]** Code push (Shorebird is named in `plan.md`). ## Open questions / decisions this raises

1. **Login set on iOS.** Offer Google sign-in plus Sign in with Apple (needs Apple provider config in Firebase, token revocation in the deletion flow), or ship email/password (or email link) only on iOS and avoid 4.8 altogether? A reviewer demo account needs a non-social login either way.
2. **Who deletes the data.** ADR 0002 says the Go service owns model calls "and nothing else", and ADR 0001 has the client own its data. Account deletion needs to remove Firestore documents, Cover photos, backend Quota/Plan records and the Auth user, reliably and also from a web page with no app installed. Options: client-side deletion under security rules plus a backend call; a deletion endpoint on the Go service; or a Firebase extension/Cloud Function triggered on Auth user deletion. The web path rules out a client-only approach. This probably warrants an ADR.
3. **Where the web deletion URL lives**, and what it is: a signed-in web page that deletes immediately, or a form/email request handled manually (Google accepts both; Apple's in-app flow must not depend on email). The same site should host the privacy policy, terms and support contact.
4. **Are allergy and diet Constraints "health data"?** Apple's label type "Health" includes "any other user provided health or medical data"; Google's "Health info" is "information about a user's health, such as medical records or symptoms". An allergy is arguably health information; "vegan" or "no seed oils" is not. Conservative choice: declare Health on both stores, used for App Functionality only. This also affects privacy-policy wording and GDPR special-category analysis (outside this research).
5. **Health apps declaration on Play:** declare "Nutrition and Weight Management" or certify no health features? Declaring brings the mandatory "not a medical device…" listing disclaimer and the "consult a healthcare professional" reminder, both cheap. Declaring nothing risks a policy strike if Google disagrees. Related: keep the App Store category as Food & Drink to avoid the medical-device declaration.
6. **Wording and placement of the macro-estimate disclaimer.** Suggested minimum: label every macro figure as "estimated", one explanatory line on the Recipe screen ("Estimated by AI from the ingredient list. Not verified; not medical or dietary advice."), and the same in both store descriptions. Decide whether the allergy notice is per-Draft, per-Recipe, or shown once at profile setup plus per-Draft.
7. **AI data-sharing consent (Apple 5.1.2(i)).** Where does the consent screen go (onboarding vs. first Generation Request), what happens if the user declines (the app's core feature is unavailable; saved Recipes still work), and what exactly is in the payload? Keeping identifiers out of the prompt is cheap and reduces what must be disclosed.
8. **Report/flag mechanism for Drafts.** Where do reports go (Firestore collection, email, issue tracker), and is the Generation Request stored with them? That storage is itself data collection to declare.
9. **Personal vs. organisation Play account.** Personal means the 12-tester/14-day closed test and public display of personal details; organisation means D-U-N-S and a registered legal entity. Also relevant to Apple DSA trader status: as a trader, a contact address and phone number are published in the EU. Decide what address and phone number to use.
10. **Shorebird.** Use it at all for v1? If yes, write down a rule for what may be patched.
11. **Free trials / introductory offers.** Offering one adds disclosure obligations on both stores; skipping it for v1 simplifies the paywall.
12. **US age-assurance laws (Texas, Utah, Louisiana).** Decide whether to take legal advice or restrict distribution; neither store mandates the APIs.
13. **iOS build pipeline without a Mac.** Which hosted macOS option builds, signs and uploads, and how are TestFlight and StoreKit sandbox purchases tested without local Apple hardware? At least one physical iPhone is practically necessary ("tested on-device", Guideline 2.1(a)).

## Unverified or not confirmed from a primary source

- That Shorebird patches qualify under Apple's "interpreted code" allowance and Google's "virtual machine or interpreter" exception. Neither store names Shorebird; only the underlying rules were verified.
- That App Review in practice accepts Apple's standard EULA link as the "Terms of Use" link for subscription apps. Schedule 2 §3.8(b) and the subscriptions page require the links; the accepted form is from reviewer practice, not a quoted rule.
- How strictly App Review applies 5.1.2(i) ("third-party AI") when the AI provider acts as the developer's processor (Vertex AI under Google Cloud terms) and no identifiers are sent. The guideline text is verified; its enforcement scope is not documented by Apple.
- Whether Google classifies a non-chatbot text generator (recipes) as in scope of the AI-Generated Content policy. The policy sentence ("Apps that generate content using AI must contain in-app user reporting…") is unqualified; the explainer's examples do not mention this type of app either way.
- Whether allergy/diet Constraints are "Health" data for Apple's label or "Health info" for Google's form. Definitions are quoted; neither source addresses food allergies.
- Exactly which developer details Google displays for a monetising personal account (the verified page says only that it "depend[s] on your account type and whether you monetize").
- The 3–4 week lead-time estimate for the Play closed test is this document's estimate (14 days of testing plus setup and production-access review); Google states only the 12 testers / 14 days rule.
